## What Rubin says (paraphrased)

- Rings of Power took **three years**, in large part because he and
  Andy Gavin were at two different universities and worked **by modem**
  between the two places. Rubin calls it a huge game that took forever
  to test. It was their first console game and their first mainstream
  game; he remains proud of it.
- When they started there were **no game-development courses and barely
  any books** on the subject — they learned by making games, by trial
  and error.
- **Andy Gavin was the leading designer** on Rings of Power and **wrote
  the code**. Rubin says he could not have as much design influence
  from 1,000 miles away, and that specific design inspirations would be
  better asked of Gavin. Rubin took over the designer role thereafter.
- The game was influenced by **dozens of games, books and other media**;
  the combination was Gavin's strong love of RPGs plus the fact that
  Gavin was the one writing the code. No detailed plot synopsis or named
  individual influences in the interview; Rubin defers specifics to Gavin.
- On Electronic Arts / publishing: Rubin describes it as a strange world
  and says he has **never quite forgiven EA** for something connected
  with that experience (stated generally, no detailed business history).
- No technical breakdown of Mega Drive hardware, cartridge size,
  graphics or sound programming in the interview. No review scores,
  sales figures or formal reception history.

## Negative finding (important)

- The word **"combat" does not appear** in the interview, and Rubin
  does not discuss a combat system or say it was reworked several times.
  The combat-rework story is **not** supported by this interview — do
  not attribute it to Rubin. Its evidence remains the in-game dialogue
  line itself: $DDBD8 record 1004, "Did someone say redo the combat a
  fifth time? I need a haircut."

## Why it matters for the project

- Authorship: Gavin = lead designer + coder; Rubin = remote collaborator.
  Design questions (including combat design intent) are Gavin's domain.
- The three-year, two-university, modem-based development explains the
  long, layered codebase — consistent with the ~5 combat revisions the
  dialogue jokes about and the vestigial code #007 is looking for.
