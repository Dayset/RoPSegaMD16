### 1a. GamePro score conflict — RESOLVED (2026-11-09, continuation session)

The earlier draft flagged 21/25 (current Wikipedia) vs 5/5 (an
older Wikipedia mirror). Verification against the magazine
itself: the Internet Archive holds a full scan of GamePro
issue 31 (Feb 1992) —
https://archive.org/stream/GamePro_Issue_031_February_1992/GamePro_Issue_031_February_1992_djvu.txt
— whose OCR text confirms the review (p.52, by Earth Angel),
its substance (massive quest, ~150 hours, gigantic manual with
a complete run-through, choppy scrolling, terrific tunes,
"a must for video adventurers") and its rating-system header
(Graphics / Sound / Gameplay / FunFactor / Challenge). The
category numerals are a printed graphic and do not survive in
the OCR layer; they are supplied by a page-by-page recap of
the same scan (https://countzeroor.com/2009/06/25/where-i-read-gamepro-31/):
**FunFactor 5, Challenge 5, Graphics 4, Sound 4, Gameplay 3 —
which sums to exactly 21/25.** Sega Retro's scan index
independently converts GamePro to **84** (21/25 × 100).
Conclusion: GamePro published no single overall score; the
aggregate is 21/25, and the older mirror's "5/5" is one of the
two 5-scoring categories mistaken for an overall mark. The
conflict is resolved in favour of 21/25; do not cite 5/5 as a
GamePro overall score.

Retrospective (kept separate from contemporary reviews):
Sega-16's 2011 review scored the game **4/10** (see #034,
`docs/research/sources.md`).

The **range** is the story: 49% to top marks. The recurring
criticisms across the retrospective record (which echoes the
contemporary ones): controls, scrolling, slow/cryptic gameplay,
unmarked map + no quest journal making the game punishingly
opaque; the recurring praise: presentation, scope, ambition.
The Genesis Temple article's known summary (slowness, cryptic
nature) is consistent with that range but cites no scores itself.

## 3. Sales / publishing facts (acceptance item 3)

- The cart was expensive to make: 1 MB ROM + save capability
  (the serial EEPROM, #019) — Genesis Temple via `docs/logic.md`
  Background + the repo's history notes.
- It **sold out its first run and EA never restocked/reprinted**
  it — Rubin's account (Genesis Temple / interview research in
  repo), echoed by retrospectives (We Play Retro: EA declined a
  reprint due to cart cost and focus on faster-selling titles).
  **Second source for "best-selling used Genesis game": none
  found** in this session's searches — that specific claim rests
  on Rubin's account alone and is recorded as single-source.
- The experience discouraged Gavin/Rubin into a hiatus from the
  industry (Wikipedia summary of the record).
- "~50,000 units" circulates in community posts — **unverified,
  do not cite** (noted in sources.md).

## 4. PROPOSED reception section for docs/overview.md (not applied)

> ### Reception (proposed, #036)
> Rings of Power shipped for Genesis in early 1992 (ROM header
> 1992.JAN; some databases list 1991 — unresolved). Contemporary
> reviews were split: GamePro praised it highly while MegaTech
> gave it 49%, criticising controls and difficulty. The expensive
> 1 MB + EEPROM cartridge sold out its first run; EA declined to
> reprint it. Its reputation since is that of an ambitious,
> opaque, cult RPG — Naughty Dog's first console game. Full
> sourcing: `docs/research/reception.md`.
